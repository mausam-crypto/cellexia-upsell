# Merchant Guide — Cellexia Post-Purchase Upsell

This guide is for the Cellexia team operating the app day to day. It explains
every page in the admin, what the defaults do, and the strategy the app was
built around. No technical knowledge required.

---

## The 60-second version

Right after a customer pays by card, they see a one-time offer for a product
that complements what they just bought. One click adds it to the same order —
same card, no extra shipping, no re-entering anything. The app decides **what**
to offer (rules you define, or an automatic "auto-pilot" ranking), **what to
say** (AI-written copy in the buyer's language), and **what discount to give**
(10–15% by default). Customers who paid with Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal or
Klarna can't see that page (a Shopify restriction), so they get a smaller
fallback offer with a personal 48-hour discount code on the thank-you page
instead.

Out of the box, with zero configuration:

- **Single-product orders** get **1** highly complementary offer.
- **Multi-product orders** get up to **3** offers, shown one at a time,
  ordered by expected gross profit per impression.
- Discounts are **tiered 10–15%**: 10% under €60, 12% from €60, 15% from €120.
- A customer sees offers at most **once every 14 days** (frequency cap).
- Products the customer already owns (bought in the last **60 days**) or that
  are in the current order are never offered.
- Copy is generated by AI in the buyer's language, with a tone that never
  implies their original purchase was wrong or incomplete.

## Dashboard (app home)

What you see:

- **KPI cards (last 30 days):** impressions (offer pages shown), acceptance
  rate, upsell revenue, gross profit, and **GP per impression** — the app's
  north-star metric (how much profit every shown offer earns on average).
- **Trend chart** of impressions and accepts over time.
- **Top 5 offers** by performance.
- **Setup checklist** — warns if the product catalog hasn't synced, if the AI
  key is missing, and reminds you that (a) the post-purchase page must be
  selected in *Settings → Checkout* in the store admin, and (b) the
  post-purchase page only appears for credit-card payments — other payment
  methods are covered by the thank-you fallback.
- **Sync** action — refreshes the product catalog, markets and languages from
  Shopify. Products update automatically via webhooks, but run this after
  bulk imports or if something looks stale.

## Offers (rules)

**Offers → list page.** Your offer rules, in evaluation order. For each
order, the app walks rules by **priority (lowest number first)** and uses the
**first rule whose trigger matches**. If nothing matches, **auto-pilot** takes
over — so an empty rules list is fine; the app always has something to show.

Each row shows the rule's status, priority, a summary of its trigger, how many
slots/products it offers, and its 30-day acceptance rate. You can toggle rules
on/off or delete them here.

**Rule editor** (Create rule / click a rule):

1. **Name, enabled, priority.** Lower priority number = checked first.
2. **Trigger — when does this rule apply?** All conditions are combined with
   AND; anything left empty means "any". Match on: specific products in the
   order, product tags, product types, number of distinct items (min/max),
   order total (min/max), destination countries.
3. **Offers.**
   - **Display mode:** *Store default* (sequential), *Sequential* (one offer
     per page, up to 3 pages), or *Bundle* (all products on one page, one
     combined pitch, one "Add all" button).
   - **Copy length:** short (default — punchy) or long (more persuasion room).
   - **Max offers:** 1–3.
   - **Three slots** — slot 1 is the first page the buyer sees, slot 2 the
     second, etc. Each slot holds one or more **candidates**: put 2+ products
     in a slot and the app A/B rotates them, learning which converts best
     (see *Rotation & winners* below). Each candidate shows its stats
     ("x/y accepted"), a weight (bias the rotation toward a product without
     turning the test off), an on/off toggle, and a **Winner** badge once the
     app has picked one.
4. **Discount override** — leave on "none" to use the global discount
   strategy, or force a fixed percentage for this rule.

> **Tip:** rules are for merchandising intent ("everyone who buys the Retinol
> Night Cream should be offered the Eye Serum first"). Leave the rest to
> auto-pilot — it's usually within a point or two of a hand-tuned rule and it
> updates itself.

### How auto-pilot ranks products (plain English)

When no rule matches, every active, in-stock, non-suppressed product is scored
on four signals:

- **Compatibility (35%)** — is it bought together with what's in the basket
  historically? Is it a different product type (cross-sell beats "another one
  of the same")? Do the tags overlap?
- **Acceptance (25%)** — how often this product has been accepted when
  offered before.
- **Repeat purchase (20%)** — do customers come back for more of it?
  (Great post-purchase offers are often replenishable.)
- **Margin (20%)** — profit after the discount.

The final ordering optimizes **gross profit per impression** by default (you
can switch to conversion or revenue in Settings → Optimization).

### Rotation & winners

When a slot has 2+ candidates, the app doesn't split traffic 50/50 forever. It
uses a bandit algorithm (Thompson sampling) that shifts traffic toward the
better performer *while* it's still learning. Once a candidate has at least
200 impressions and the app is ≥95% sure it's the best (both thresholds
adjustable in Settings → Optimization), it's marked **Winner** and gets the
traffic — except for a 10% exploration share that keeps checking whether
things changed. You can reset the experiment stats or force a winner-pick from
Settings.

## Prompts (AI copy)

Three templates, one per situation:

- **Single product** — the classic one-offer page.
- **Bundle** — one combined pitch for a set of products.
- **Sequenced offers** — page N of M; instructed not to repeat the previous
  pages' angles.

For each you can edit the **system prompt** (the persona/rules) and the
**user prompt** (the request), pick the **model** — `claude-haiku-4-5` (fast,
default) or `claude-sonnet-5` (best) — and tune the max tokens.
Saving bumps the template's version, which invalidates the copy cache so new
copy is generated.

**Template variables** you can use in prompts (the page also documents them):

| Variable | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `{{brand_context}}` | Brand voice text from Settings → General |
| `{{tone}}` | Tone instruction from Settings |
| `{{language}}` | The buyer's language — copy is written in it |
| `{{length}}` | short / long |
| `{{basket_summary}}` | What the customer just bought, e.g. "2× Retinol Night Cream (Cream); 1× Vitamin C Serum (Serum)" |
| `{{offer_summary}}` | The offered product(s): title, type, price, short description |
| `{{discount_pct}}` | The discount for this offer |
| `{{currency}}` | Order currency |
| `{{position}}` / `{{total_offers}}` | "Offer 2 of 3" context for sequenced flows |

**Preview** — pick a language and generate a sample using real catalog
products, so you can see headline/body/bullets (and generation latency) before
buyers do.

Guardrails baked into the default prompts (keep them if you rewrite): write in
the buyer's language; concrete benefit tied to what's in the basket; **never**
imply the original purchase was wrong or incomplete; no medical claims; no
emojis; mention the discount naturally.

If the AI is unavailable or slow (over ~2.5s), buyers see clean generic
fallback copy instead of waiting — and the real copy is generated in the
background for the next buyer. Nothing ever blocks the checkout.

## Settings

Each section saves independently.

- **General** — master on/off, thank-you fallback on/off, offers per order:
  **1 for single-product orders, up to 3 for multi-product orders**
  (the defaults; hard maximum is 3 — a Shopify limit), default display mode
  (sequential), brand context and tone fed to the AI, compare-at price
  display, countdown (default on, 10 minutes).
- **Discount** — the global strategy:
  - *Fixed*: always the same %.
  - *Tiered* (default): by order value — 10% / 12% from €60 / 15% from €120.
  - *AI-adjusted*: Claude proposes a % within your min–max band per offer.
  - Whatever the mode, everything is clamped to your **min (10%) – max (15%)**
    band. Staying in 10–15% is deliberate: enough to convert, not enough to
    train customers to wait for discounts or to gut margin.
- **Frequency & hygiene** — frequency cap (default **14 days** between offers
  per customer), suppression window (don't offer what they bought in the last
  **60 days**), minimum inventory to be offerable (default 1).
- **Optimization** — the metric to optimize (default **gross profit per
  impression**), rotation settings (exploration %, impressions needed before a
  winner can be picked, confidence threshold, auto-pick on/off), plus
  **Reset experiment stats** and **Pick winners now** buttons.
- **Markets** — one row per Shopify Market: enable/disable offers there,
  override the discount %, force a language, cap the number of offers.
  **Re-sync** pulls new markets from Shopify without touching your overrides.
- **Languages** — which store languages the app serves and the default. These
  come from Shopify's published locales; all 17 Cellexia languages are
  supported.
- **AI** — AI copy on/off, model, timeout, and the translation provider
  (Claude or DeepL) used for the static UI strings. A banner warns if the
  Anthropic API key is missing on the server.

## Translations

The **offer copy** (headline, body, bullets) is generated per-language by the
AI — you don't translate it. This page manages the **static strings** around
it: buttons ("Add to my order", "No thanks, complete my order"), price labels
("Was/Now/Save x%"), the countdown label, the thank-you page texts, etc.

Pick a language, edit any string (missing ones show the English placeholder),
and Save. **Auto-translate missing** fills gaps via AI; **Re-translate all**
redoes the language from scratch. Worth a native-speaker skim for your top
languages — these strings sit right next to the buy button.

## Analytics

Use the period selector (7 / 30 / 90 days) and **Export CSV** for offline
analysis.

**Definitions:**

- **Impression** — one offer page shown to a buyer.
- **Accept / Decline** — the buyer's click on that page.
- **Acceptance rate** — accepts ÷ impressions. Healthy post-purchase flows
  run roughly 3–10%; a great targeted offer can beat that.
- **Upsell revenue** — discounted price of accepted items.
- **Gross profit** — revenue minus product cost (uses your cost-per-item from
  Shopify; where cost is missing, it's estimated at 55% of price).
- **GP per impression** — gross profit ÷ impressions. The single best number
  for comparing offers: it balances how often an offer converts against how
  much profit each conversion carries.
- **Offers per order shown** — average pages a buyer saw per order.

**Sections:**

- **Funnel** — impressions → accepts → revenue for the period.
- **Offer performance** — per product: impressions, accepts, acceptance rate,
  revenue, GP, GP/impression, average discount. Sort by GP/impression to find
  what to scale; a high-acceptance low-GP row means the discount is too deep
  or the product too cheap.
- **Breakdowns** — by country, language, and surface (post-purchase vs
  thank-you). Use these to spot markets that deserve an override (Settings →
  Markets) — e.g. a country converting at half the average might need a
  different product or a bigger discount.
- **Experiments** — every rotation slot with 2+ candidates: each candidate's
  stats plus **P(best)**, the probability it's truly the best. Winner badges
  appear when auto-pick fires.
- **CLV cohorts (60 and 90 days)** — the strategic view. Customers are
  grouped by their first offer experience: **accepted**, **declined** (saw
  offers, never accepted), or **not shown**. For each cohort you see how much
  they spent and how often they ordered in the following 60/90 days,
  *excluding* the original order. **This is the number the whole app is
  aimed at**: a good upsell program grows what customers spend over the next
  quarter, not just the day-one order. If accepters meaningfully out-spend the
  others, the program is compounding; if declined customers under-spend
  "not shown" ones, you're showing too many or too aggressive offers — raise
  the frequency cap or tighten targeting.

## Strategy playbook (defaults and why)

1. **Keep discounts at 10–15%.** Below 10% rarely moves post-purchase
   conversion; above 15% erodes margin and trains discount-waiting. Tiered by
   order value (the default) rewards bigger baskets.
2. **One offer for single-product orders.** A first-time or focused buyer gets
   one great complement, not a gauntlet. Multi-product buyers have shown
   basket-building intent — up to 3 sequenced offers is appropriate there.
3. **Sequential beats bundle as the default.** One decision per page converts
   better; use **bundle** mode deliberately for true regimens (e.g. a
   day/night routine) where the combined story is the pitch.
4. **Respect the frequency cap (14 days).** The post-purchase moment works
   because it's rare and relevant. Hammering repeat buyers shows up as
   declining CLV in the declined cohort.
5. **Let rotation pick winners.** Give a slot 2–3 plausible candidates and
   let Thompson sampling decide with real traffic (auto-winner at 200+
   impressions / 95% confidence by default). Resist calling winners by eye at
   50 impressions.
6. **Judge by GP per impression and 60/90-day CLV**, not acceptance rate
   alone. A 12% acceptance on a €19 product can be worth less than 5% on a
   €78 cream.
7. **Localize per market where data says so.** Use Settings → Markets for
   discount/language/volume overrides, and the country/language breakdowns to
   find where.
8. **Keep costs filled in Shopify** (product cost per item) — gross-profit
   ranking and reporting are only as good as your cost data.
9. **Remember the payment-method split.** Only card payments see the
   post-purchase page (Shopify platform rule). Wallet/PayPal buyers get the
   thank-you fallback with a 48-hour single-use code — expect lower volume
   from that surface; it's a bonus channel, not the main act.
10. **Mind the copy guardrails.** The AI is instructed to never suggest the
    customer bought the wrong thing and to make no medical claims — keep those
    rules in any prompt edits; they're brand protection, not style.
